# Usability Task Script: Ovie Home (`index.html`)

Moderator guide for one 45-minute session. Read the quoted text aloud. Do not read the bracketed notes.

Persona data in the prototype: Rishi Verma, 5 policies (2022 Honda CR-V, Austin home, Family umbrella, Downtown apartment, 2018 Toyota Camry), $4,860 per year.

## Setup checklist

- Recording works, with consent confirmed verbally.
- Prototype open on a phone at `index.html`, freshly loaded for each task start (reload so no drawers or accordions are open).
- Participant knows: we are testing the design, not them. There are no wrong answers. Some parts may not work yet.
- Timer, scoring sheet and SEQ question ready.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. "Tell me a bit about yourself and what you do day to day."
2. "Do you have insurance such as car, home or renters? Roughly how many policies?"

## Pre-test (4 min)

1. "How do you keep track of your insurance today? Where do the documents live?"
2. "When did you last look at one of your policies, and what were you trying to find out?"
3. "Have you used an app or website that gathers all your insurance in one place?"

## Think-aloud prompt

"As you go, please say out loud what you're looking at, what you expect to happen, and anything that surprises you. I may not answer questions right away, because I want to see what you'd do on your own."

---

## Task 1: Orientation (3 min)

**Say:** "You've just opened this app on your phone. Take a moment and tell me what you think this screen is for, and whether there's anything it wants you to do."

**Do not say:** "policies", "insights", "needs your attention", "scroll".
**Success:** Describes it as an overview of their insurance, and identifies at least one flagged item.
**Watch for:** Whether they notice the amber "Needs your attention" card, what they think the bell dot means, whether they scroll, what they call the sections.
**Probes (after):** "What would you tap first?" "What do you think the red dot on the bell means?"
**Post-task:** SEQ: "Overall, how easy or difficult was this task?" (1 very difficult to 7 very easy.) "What was easy, and what was confusing?"

## Task 2: Find a date and a cost (4 min)

**Say:** "Your car insurance is with State Farm. You want to know exactly when the Honda's coverage runs out and what you pay for it each year. Show me how you'd find that."

**Expected path:** "Your policies", tap 2022 Honda CR-V, read period and annual premium. A path through "Your premium breakdown" is also valid.
**Success:** States the Honda ends Jan 20, 2027 and costs $1,320 per year.
**Assistance rule:** After 60 seconds with no progress, ask "What would you expect to tap?" If still stuck after 30 more seconds, mark as assisted and show the policy list.
**Watch for:** Which section they use, whether they notice the expiry date on the closed row, whether they see the monthly equivalent.
**Post-task:** SEQ and "What was easy, and what was confusing?"

## Task 3: Respond to a warning (5 min)

**Say:** "Something on this screen suggests there's a problem with the Toyota Camry's insurance. Find out what it is and show me what you would do about it."

**Do not say:** "state minimum", "coverage", "tap the card".
**Expected path:** Tap the "State minimum coverage not established" row, read the toast, then look for the Camry policy (requires "See all 5").
**Success levels:**
- Found and understood the warning (read the toast).
- Went on to open the Camry policy.
- Stated a next step in their own words.

**Prototype note:** The warning shows only as a toast and nothing navigates. Do not rescue. After 90 seconds, ask: "What did you expect to happen when you tapped that?"
**Watch for:** Whether the toast is read before it disappears, whether the participant taps again, whether they find "See all 5", and how they describe the warning in their own words.
**Probes (after):** "What does that message mean to you?" "How worried are you about it?" "Where would you want to go next?"
**Post-task:** SEQ and "What was easy, and what was confusing?"

## Task 4: Understand spending (5 min)

**Say:** "You want to know how much of your insurance spending goes to your cars, and which policies that includes. Show me how you'd work that out."

**Expected path:** "Your premium breakdown", tap Auto, read the percentage and the list of vehicles.
**Success:** States Auto is $2,040 per year (42%) and names the Honda and the Camry.
**Watch for:** Taps on the donut chart itself, whether they read the centre number, whether the percentage or the dollar figure is what they use, and whether they understand the centre value changing.
**Probes (after):** "What did the centre of the chart show after you tapped?" "Would you expect to tap the chart itself?"
**Post-task:** SEQ and "What was easy, and what was confusing?"

## Task 5: Add a document (4 min)

**Say:** "Your home insurer, Travelers, just emailed you the renewed policy. You want it to show up in this app. Show me how you'd do that."

**Expected paths:** Tap the centre Upload button in the bottom bar, or open the profile drawer and use "My Ovie Inbox" to forward the email.
**Success:** Reaches the file picker, or finds and understands the inbox address.
**Prototype note:** After the file picker, the prototype shows a toast. Treat reaching the picker as success.
**Watch for:** Whether they identify the icon-only Upload button, whether they check My Files first, and whether they notice the inbox address in the profile drawer.
**Probes (after):** "What did you expect that button to do?" "If I told you there's an email address for this, where would you look for it?"
**Post-task:** SEQ and "What was easy, and what was confusing?"

## Optional Task 6: Compare quotes (3 min)

**Say:** "You're thinking about switching your car insurance and have a couple of quotes. Find a way to compare them."

**Expected path:** More, then "Compare Quotes".
**Success:** Opens Compare Quotes.
**Watch for:** Whether they try Insights, Ask Ovie or Search first.

---

## Debrief (5 to 8 min)

1. "In your own words, what is this app for?"
2. "What did you like most? What was most frustrating?"
3. "Were there any buttons or labels you weren't sure about?" Point to the bottom bar if needed: "What do you think each of these does?"
4. "The floating chat button: what do you think it does, and would you use it?"
5. "Would you use this to manage your insurance? What would need to be true?"
6. Optional: SUS questionnaire.

## Close (2 min)

Thank them, confirm the incentive, remind them the recording is confidential.

## Moderator rules

- Talk about 20% of the time. Do not lead: never say "are you looking for the menu?".
- Do not defend the design or explain stubs mid-task.
- Note exact words, hesitations, back-tracking and emotional reactions.
- Treat participant feature ideas as signals about the problem, not as requirements.
